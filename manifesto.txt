leading-tight font-light md:text-5xl">Getting customers is an engineering problem now.</h1><p class="text-muted-foreground text-xl leading-relaxed">Every year there are more places to show up and the same number of hours to do it in. Something had to give.</p></header><div class="prose prose-lg dark:prose-invert max-w-none"><p class="text-muted-foreground mb-6 leading-relaxed">Every year there&#x27;s a new place you&#x27;re supposed to show up. Search, then social, then video, then newsletters, then communities, and now AI that answers people&#x27;s questions before they ever click a link. None of the old ones disappear when a new one arrives. They just stack.</p><p class="text-muted-foreground mb-6 leading-relaxed">So the list of things that supposedly work keeps getting longer while the day stays exactly as long as it&#x27;s always been. For most of the internet&#x27;s history there was one answer to that gap, and it was brute force. More people. More tools. More hours. Hope something catches.</p><p class="text-muted-foreground mb-6 leading-relaxed">What actually changed has nothing to do with the size of the pile. Software can finally think. An agent can look at what&#x27;s happening, decide what to do about it, go do it, see whether it worked, and try something else when it didn&#x27;t. That&#x27;s a different animal than a tool that just drafts faster. It can run a whole play from start to finish and change its mind in the middle.</p><p class="text-muted-foreground mb-6 leading-relaxed">Which quietly turns growth from a willpower problem into a design one. The interesting work moves up a level: from doing the task to building the system that does the task from now on. We started calling that Growth Engineering.</p><p class="text-muted-foreground mb-6 leading-relaxed">It&#x27;s mostly a way of looking at things. Take some repetitive marketing grind, the kind everyone writes off as the cost of doing business, and instead of accepting it, treat it as a system waiting to be built. Then build it, usually before anyone thinks to ask.</p><p class="text-muted-foreground mb-6 leading-relaxed">In practice it&#x27;s quiet. Systems running in the background that nobody really sees. Something watching the conversations happening in your corner of the internet and stepping in where it genuinely fits. Something that catches a customer in the exact moment they&#x27;re happy and asks for the review. Something keeping score across every channel at once and moving effort toward whatever&#x27;s pulling its weight.</p><p class="text-muted-foreground mb-6 leading-relaxed">None of that needed another app. It needed a different way of working, and that only became possible the moment a machine could reason its way through a real workflow instead of just following the steps it was handed.</p><p class="text-muted-foreground mb-6 leading-relaxed">The people figuring this out first usually aren&#x27;t the biggest teams or the loudest accounts. They&#x27;re whoever got tired of treating distribution like a chore to survive and started treating it like something worth building. Everything we make is for them.</p></div></article></main><footer class="w-full py-12 md:py-16"><div class="mx-auto max-w-[1800px] px-6 sm:px-10 lg:px-16"><div class="flex flex-col gap-10 md:flex-row
